What this is

Vrai Québec — "Real Quebec" — is an independent travel guide to the province beyond its two big cities. We cover the regions travelers actually ask about: the Laurentians, the Eastern Townships, Charlevoix, and the Gaspé Peninsula. We publish complete road-trip itineraries with day-by-day detail, and a practical outdoors guide to the parks, hikes, ski hills, and whale-watching that make Quebec one of North America's great outdoor destinations.

What we cover

Right now the guide has four region guides and two road-trip itineraries, plus the outdoors guide:

  • The Laurentians — Mont-Tremblant, Saint-Sauveur, Parc national du Mont-Tremblant, fall colours, and skiing.
  • Eastern Townships — Magog and Lac Memphrémagog, Mont-Orford, Sutton, and the Route des vins.
  • Charlevoix — Baie-Saint-Paul, La Malbaie, the Hautes-Gorges and the Acropole des Draveurs, Le Massif.
  • Gaspésie — Percé, Île-Bonaventure, Parc national de la Gaspésie, Forillon, and Route 132.
  • Tour de la Gaspésie — the full Route 132 loop, day by day, five to seven days.
  • Laurentians Weekend — two or three days from Montreal.
  • Outdoors — SEPAQ parks, the great hikes, downhill and backcountry skiing, whale watching, and the P'tit Train du Nord.

Each region page includes an interactive map so you can orient yourself before you go. We plan to add more regions and itineraries over time — the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean, the Bas-Saint-Laurent, and Quebec's north are all on the list.

Corrections

Roads close, park fees change, ferry schedules shift. If you spot something out of date, tell us at info@montrealguide.ca and we'll fix it.

How we work

Everything here is written by people who know Quebec firsthand — who have driven Route 132 around the peninsula, hiked the Acropole des Draveurs, cycled the P'tit Train du Nord, and watched the sun set behind Rocher Percé. We don't run sponsored rankings and we don't sell placements. When we recommend a route, a park, or a season, it's because we'd send a friend there.

We keep the guide practical on purpose: how to get there, when to go, what it costs in effort, and what to watch out for. Conditions change — park hours, road work, ferry schedules — so we always recommend confirming details with SEPAQ, Parks Canada, and local tourism offices before you travel.
