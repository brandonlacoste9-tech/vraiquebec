Tour de la Gaspésie

The full Route 132 loop around the Gaspé Peninsula — five to seven days, clockwise from Sainte-Flavie.

Home / Road Trips / Tour de la Gaspésie

This is the one. The Tour de la Gaspésie — the loop of Route 132 around the entire peninsula — is widely considered the finest road trip in Quebec, and it belongs on any serious list of great Canadian drives. Roughly 900 kilometres of driving, but the driving is the point: the road rarely leaves the water on the north shore and never stops surprising on the south. Go clockwise, starting at Sainte-Flavie where the 132 splits, so the sea stays on your right and the pull-offs come naturally.

Five days is the honest minimum; seven lets you hike, linger, and absorb a rainy day without stress. Summer is peak season — book Percé lodging and SEPAQ park stays well ahead. September is the connoisseur's choice: thinner crowds, the start of colour in the Chic-Chocs, and the sea at its warmest.

Day 1 — Sainte-Flavie to Sainte-Anne-des-Monts

Pick up the 132 at Sainte-Flavie and head east along the north shore. The first stretch is gentle farmland giving way to wilder coast. Stop at Matane for the shrimp — the town's crevettes de Matane are a genuine local institution — and walk the waterfront. Push on to Sainte-Anne-des-Monts, the gateway town for Parc national de la Gaspésie, and settle in. If you arrive early, drive up toward the park entrance at dusk for your first look at the Chic-Chocs.

Day 2 — Parc national de la Gaspésie

Give the mountains a full day. The classic objective is Mont Jacques-Cartier: a demanding full-day hike through boreal forest into alpine tundra, with a real chance of seeing woodland caribou at the summit — the last herd south of the far north. Shorter on time or legs? The Mont Ernest-Laforce trail offers tundra views on a gentler profile. Either way, start early, carry layers (the summit weather is a different season), and check conditions at the park's discovery centre. Sleep again in Sainte-Anne-des-Monts or move east toward Rivière-au-Renard.

Day 3 — Forillon and the tip

Continue east to Forillon National Park, where the Appalachians end at the sea. The Cap-des-Rosiers lighthouse — the tallest in Canada — anchors the north side; the Les Graves trail on the south side ends at Land's End, a headland with nothing but ocean beyond. Watch for seals hauled out on the rocks and, in season, whales offshore. Overnight in Gaspé town, a working port with good seafood and a slower evening pace than Percé.

Day 4 — Gaspé to Percé

The short hop south to Percé is the emotional centre of the trip. Arrive by midday, walk the waterfront, and take the boat to Île-Bonaventure in the afternoon — the gannet colony is one of the great wildlife sights in North America, tens of thousands of birds on sea cliffs you can walk right up to. Back in Percé, watch the sun drop behind Rocher Percé from the beach. Two nights here is the move if you have seven days; one is the minimum.

Day 5 — The Baie des Chaleurs

Leave Percé heading west along the south shore. The Baie des Chaleurs is the trip's warm exhale: stop in Bonaventure for the Bioparc if you're travelling with kids, then Carleton-sur-Mer — walk the sandbar, drive up Mont-Saint-Joseph for the bay panorama. The villages along this shore (New Richmond, Maria, Carleton) have a distinct Acadian character and some of the peninsula's best smoked fish and microbreweries. Overnight in Carleton-sur-Mer.

Days 6–7 — The Matapédia valley and home

From Carleton, Route 132 turns inland up the Matapédia valley — salmon rivers, covered bridges, and quiet farmland, a complete change of scenery from the coast. Amqui and Causapscal are the valley towns; the salmon interpretation centres are worth a stop in season. Rejoin the main highway at Mont-Joli and close the loop back at Sainte-Flavie, or continue west toward home. With seven days, spend the extra night wherever the weather was kindest to you.

Practical notes

  • Direction: clockwise from Sainte-Flavie keeps the sea on your right and the viewpoints easy.
  • Fuel: fill up in the bigger towns — Matane, Sainte-Anne-des-Monts, Gaspé, Percé, Carleton-sur-Mer. Stations thin out between them.
  • Parks: SEPAQ (Gaspésie) and Parks Canada (Forillon) both charge entry; book campsites and huts ahead in summer.
  • Whales: the north shore and Forillon offer the best chances, June through October — bring binoculars.
  • Language: French dominates, but the tourist corridor is used to English speakers. The south shore has deep Acadian roots.

The Gaspé doesn't do rushed. Build slack into every day — for the lighthouse you didn't know about, the chip stand with the perfect view, the evening you end up watching the sun go down instead of driving. That's not lost time on this loop. That's the loop.
